# SEAMUN I 2027 RoP (Extracted Reference)

This file extracts the key rules and lists from **SEAMUN I 2027: Rules of Procedure (RoP)** into a site-usable reference (no full DOCX reproduction).

## 1) Delegate Conduct & Language (conference rules)

### Professional respect
- Absolute respect must be shown to all attendees: fellow delegates, advisors, chairs, Secretariat staff, and external providers.

### Political neutrality
- Personal political activism must be kept outside the conference; debates should focus on the positions represented rather than personal beliefs.

### Hate speech & harassment
- Hate speech of any kind is strictly prohibited (including "ironic" use or jokes).
- Language that targets identity or background will result in immediate disciplinary action.

### Formal vocabulary
- Slang, "brain rot" terminology, and meme references are prohibited during formal sessions.
- Informal language is permitted during breaks and unmoderated caucuses, but excessive use may negatively impact award eligibility.

### Language policy
- English is the primary language of the conference.
- Inappropriate language (including profanity, innuendo, and intentional use of derogatory slurs) is prohibited.

## 2) Disciplinary System (warnings & strikes)

SEAMUN I uses a tiered system:

### Warnings
- Delegates receive up to three warnings for minor infractions or breaches of decorum.

### Strike 1
- After three warnings, a delegate receives their first strike and loses all voting rights for the remainder of the session.

### Strike 2
- A second strike suspends speaking rights in formal sessions.

### Strike 3
- A third strike results in immediate removal from the committee and potentially from the conference premises.

## 3) Committee Regulations

- Cross-talk is prohibited during formal debate.
- Communication must be conducted through the Chair, during unmoderated caucuses, or via Note Passing.

### Note passing
- Notes are for professional coordination.
- Inappropriate or harassing notes are flagged, reported to the Secretariat, and result in an immediate warning or strike.

### Parliamentary points
- Points of Parliamentary Inquiry or Personal Privilege may be raised at any time and must be recognised by the Chair at the earliest opportunity.

### Position papers (awards eligibility)
- Submission is required to be eligible for awards.
- While AI may be used for pre-conference research, generating the paper's text using AI is strictly prohibited.

### Diplomatic consistency
- Delegates must maintain their assigned country/character stance at all times.
- Deviating from an assigned stance due to personal bias results in disqualification from all awards.

## 4) Technology & Tools (AI + props)

### AI policy
Delegates:
- Strictly prohibited from using AI to generate speeches or content during the conference (including breaks).
- Break-time research may use AI briefs or AI summaries, but delegates may not conduct back-and-forth responses.
- Reports of AI use may be submitted privately to the Chair via note.
- AI use may result in automatic disqualification from all awards.

Chairs:
- Permitted to use AI tools for administrative and organisational purposes.

Props:
- Permitted only if inanimate, non-disruptive, and relevant to the committee theme.
- Objects interpreted as weapons (including toys) are strictly banned.
- Chairs must approve all objects in advance.

### Pre-lobbying
- Formal lobbying and drafting clauses before the conference are strictly prohibited.

## 5) Food & External Services

- Food delivery services are a privilege, not a right.

Group orders:
- Must include at least 4 individuals.
- Must be organised by Committee Chairs.

Logistics:
- Payments must be made online.
- Secretariat staff must be notified via a tracking link immediately upon ordering.
- The order is collected at the entrance.

Limitations:
- Committees are limited to two group orders per day.
- Secretariat can confiscate or cancel orders that disrupt the schedule.

## 6) Short Form / procedural baselines (rules of procedure)

### Validity of the RoP
- Governing authority for all simulated **non-crisis** committees.
- In specialised bodies (e.g., UNSC), these rules apply unless superseded by the Crisis Team’s specific mandates.

### Majorities
- Simple majority: 50% of present delegates plus 1.
- Two-thirds majority: 66% of present delegates plus 1.

## 7) Roll Call (long form)

Chairs conduct roll call at the start of every session:

- **Present**: in the room; delegate may choose to "Abstain" during substantive voting.
- **Present and Voting**: delegate waives abstention; must vote "Yes" or "No" on all substantive matters.
- **Absent**: recorded by the chairs when a delegate is not in the room.
- Delegates may change stance via note to the chairs.

## 10) Resolutions & amendments (long form)

### Resolution types
- Working Paper: informal draft; not yet vetted by the Dais.
- Draft Resolution: meets 15% signatory requirement and is approved by the Secretariat for formatting and substance.

### Resolution requirements
- Resolutions must have:
  - 2 main submitters
  - 2 co-submitters
  - at least 15% of the committee as signatories

### Amendments
- Friendly: supported by all main submitters; incorporated automatically upon Chair approval.
- Unfriendly: not supported by all main submitters; requires a specific debate (one speaker for, one against) and a simple majority vote.

### Amendments explicitly not permitted
- Deleting an entire resolution.
- Replacing an entire resolution.
- Adding an entire resolution.

## 11) Voting procedure (long form)

### When voting begins
- Once a Motion to Close Debate passes, the committee enters "Voting Procedure."

### Voting procedure constraints
- Lockout: no one may enter or leave the room.
- Silence: note-passing and talking are suspended.
- Administrative actions: Chair clears outstanding amendments and "Motions to Divide the Question".
- During voting procedure, breaks are not permitted.

### Voting methods
- Show of placards (standard).
- Roll call vote (a delegate may motion for this).

### Voting order for every draft resolution
For each draft resolution, voting includes three steps in this order:
1. Voting on amendments
2. Voting on motions for a division of the question
3. Voting on the draft resolution as a whole
